# Bibliography

1. **Ganin et al., 2016, _JMLR_** – *Domain-Adversarial Training of Neural Networks*. Uses a **gradient-reversal layer** for adversarial training: the feature extractor is trained to fool a domain classifier, encouraging domain-invariant features.  Achieves e.g. **73.0%** accuracy on Amazon→Webcam (Office-31 dataset), vs 64.2% without adaptation. *Relevance:* Classic UDA method directly aligning source (lab) and target (field) feature distributions. THESIS-QUOTE: “Domain-Adversarial Neural Networks update thus works adversarially to the domain classifier, and it encourages domain-invariant features to emerge in the course of the optimization.”

2. **Sun & Saenko, 2016, *ECCV Workshops*** – *Deep CORAL: Correlation Alignment for Deep Domain Adaptation*. Extends CORAL by aligning **second-order feature covariances** in deep networks. It “aligns correlations of layer activations” between source and target. On the VisDA synthetic-to-real challenge, Deep CORAL raises accuracy from 28.1% (no adaptation) to **45.5%**. *Relevance:* Matches dataset statistics in feature space, useful when field images have different style/texture. THESIS-QUOTE: “CORAL is a *‘frustratingly easy’* unsupervised domain adaptation method that aligns the second-order statistics of the source and target distributions … we extend CORAL to learn a nonlinear transformation that aligns correlations of layer activations in deep neural networks (Deep CORAL).”

3. **Long et al., 2018, *NeurIPS*** – *Conditional Adversarial Domain Adaptation (CDAN)*. Combines adversarial domain alignment with **conditioning on classifier predictions**. It uses multilinear conditioning (cross-covariance between features and predicted classes) and entropy weighting to focus on transferable examples. On Office-31 (ResNet features), CDAN achieves ~**90%** average accuracy across domain pairs. *Relevance:* Improves on DANN by preserving class structure, relevant for multi-class tasks like plant diseases. THESIS-QUOTE: “We present conditional adversarial domain adaptation (CDAN) with two novel conditioning strategies: multilinear conditioning (capturing correlations between deep features and class predictions) and entropy conditioning (focusing on confident samples). Our approach has exceeded state-of-the-art results on five datasets.”

4. **Liang et al., 2020, *ICML*** – *Source Hypothesis Transfer (SHOT) for SFDA*. A **source-free** UDA method: freeze the pretrained source classifier (“hypothesis”) and adapt only features on unlabeled target data via **self-supervised pseudo-labeling** and information maximization. SHOT “freezes the classifier module of the source model and learns the target feature extractor by exploiting information maximization and self-supervised pseudo-labeling”. It obtains new SOTA: e.g., **71.8%** on Office-Home (closed-set UDA) vs 67.6% prior. *Relevance:* No source images needed at adaptation time, matching our lab-to-field where only a pretrained model (e.g. EfficientNet on PlantVillage) is available. THESIS-QUOTE: “We propose Source Hypothesis Transfer (SHOT): freezing the source classifier and aligning target features to it via information maximization and pseudo-label self-supervision.”

5. **Wang et al., 2021, *ICLR*** – *Tent: Fully Test-Time Adaptation by Entropy Minimization*. Adapts a trained model online by minimizing prediction **entropy** on each test batch. Only unlabeled test data are used. It updates only BatchNorm affine parameters (or entire layers) to minimize confidence-based loss. Tent yields *state-of-the-art* robustness: e.g., it achieves the **new best error on ImageNet-C** (corrupted ImageNet) in a single epoch of test-time adaptation. *Relevance:* Directly adapts a lab-trained model to field images at deployment, without any labeled field data. THESIS-QUOTE: “We propose to adapt by *test entropy minimization* (Tent): optimize model confidence on each test batch. Tent significantly reduces error on corrupted ImageNet and reaches a new state-of-the-art on ImageNet-C.”

6. **Sun et al., 2020, *ICML*** – *Test-Time Training (TTT) with Self-Supervision*. At test time, performs an auxiliary self-supervised task (predict image rotations) to update model parameters on each test sample or batch. The model is improved before making the actual prediction. TTT greatly improves robustness: e.g. on CIFAR-10-C corruptions, TTT reduced error by *up to 38%* relative over the baseline. *Relevance:* Like Tent, TTT adjusts a lab-trained model online to better handle field-like corruptions or shifts. THESIS-QUOTE: “We turn a single unlabeled test sample into a self-supervised learning problem (e.g. predict image rotation) and update model parameters before prediction. This approach leads to substantial improvements under distribution shift.”

7. **Li et al., 2016, *ICCV*** – *Adaptive Batch Normalization (AdaBN)*. A simple but effective method: recompute or “modulate” BN layer mean/variance on target data. The hypothesis is that BN statistics capture domain-specific style, so replacing source BN stats with target-domain stats aligns distributions. No new parameters or training required. This “domain-specific normalization” achieves *state-of-the-art* DA results despite its simplicity. *Relevance:* Straightforward to implement for lab-to-field: just run target images through BN layers to recalibrate. THESIS-QUOTE: “We propose Adaptive Batch Normalization (AdaBN) … By modulating the statistics in all BN layers, our method achieves deep adaptation effect. It is parameter-free and achieves state-of-the-art performance despite its simplicity.”

8. **Hoffman et al., 2018, *ICML*** – *CyCADA: Cycle-Consistent Adversarial Domain Adaptation*. Performs **pixel-level style transfer** with CycleGAN plus feature-level adversarial alignment. CyCADA translates source images to target style (and vice versa) while enforcing consistency of semantic labels. Demonstrated on synthetic→real tasks, it “advances state-of-the-art performance for unsupervised adaptation from synthetic to real driving domains”. *Relevance:* Can adapt clean lab images to photorealistic “field-style” images (or vice versa). Useful when background/lighting differ. THESIS-QUOTE: “We propose CyCADA, which adapts between domains using both generative (pixel-space) and feature-space alignment. Evaluated on digit recognition and road-scene segmentation, it advances state-of-the-art in synthetic→real adaptation.”

9. **Hendrycks et al., 2020, *ICLR*** – *AugMix: Improving Robustness via Randomized Augmentations*. A data augmentation scheme combining random transformations (e.g. JPEG compression, contrast, etc.) in a mixture to train models resilient to unforeseen corruptions. AugMix “significantly improves robustness and uncertainty measures” on image benchmarks. *Relevance:* Training on aggressively augmented lab images (AugMix) helps generalize to varied field conditions (lighting, occlusion). THESIS-QUOTE: “AugMix is a simple data processing technique that uses random augmentation chains and mixes them. It significantly improves robustness to image corruptions, closing the gap with the best possible performance in some cases by more than half.”

10. **Tobin et al., 2017, *IROS*** – *Domain Randomization for Sim-to-Real Transfer*. A robotics paper: randomizes colors, textures, lighting in simulation so that real images appear as just another random variation. Trained solely on synthetic data, a model localized real objects to within **1.5cm accuracy** (sufficient for grasping). *Relevance:* Analogous to training on lab images with randomized backgrounds/styles so that field images fall within the randomization distribution. THESIS-QUOTE: “We explore domain randomization: a model is trained on only simulated images with non-realistic random textures. With enough variability, the real world appears as just another variation. We achieve a real-world object detector accurate to 1.5cm using only simulation data.”

11. **Arjovsky et al., 2020, *ICLR*** – *Invariant Risk Minimization (IRM)*. A domain-generalization paradigm: train on multiple source “environments” so that the learned representation admits a single classifier that is optimal for all environments. IRM formalizes this via an invariance constraint. It aims to capture causally invariant features for **out-of-distribution generalization**. *Relevance:* By enforcing feature-label relationships invariant across lab-like conditions, the model may better generalize to field distributions. THESIS-QUOTE: “Invariant Risk Minimization (IRM) is a learning paradigm to estimate invariant correlations across multiple training distributions. … The invariances learned by IRM relate to the causal structures in data and enable out-of-distribution generalization.”

12. **Sagawa et al., 2020, *ICML*** – *Distributionally Robust Optimization (GroupDRO)*. Optimizes the worst-case (group) loss instead of average loss. Here “groups” are subpopulations (e.g. types of field conditions). Naively applying DRO to neural nets may fail without regularization; with stronger regularization, GroupDRO achieved **10–40 percentage point improvements** on worst-group accuracy in vision tasks. *Relevance:* Encourages performance uniformity across different backgrounds/lighting, mitigating lab-field drop. THESIS-QUOTE: “Overparameterized networks can achieve high average accuracy yet fail on atypical groups. Group DRO minimizes the worst-case training loss over pre-defined groups. We show that with proper regularization, group DRO yields substantially higher worst-group accuracies (10–40% improvements) while maintaining high average accuracy.”

13. **Zhao et al., 2026, *Sensors (Basel)*** – *Target-BN Adaptation for Plant Disease (PlantVillage→PlantDoc)*. Addresses the PlantVillage (clean lab) → PlantDoc (field) gap. Their method (training-time target-BN adaptation) on EfficientNet-B0 achieves **54.2%** top-1 accuracy on PlantDoc (5-shot) compared to 42.7% one-shot. Crucially, adding DANN or test-time shifts gave inconsistent gains – BN calibration did most of the work. *Relevance:* Directly studies lab-to-field in agriculture; shows simple BN statistics recalibration (a variant of AdaBN) can reduce the gap. THESIS-QUOTE: “Under the PlantVillage-to-PlantDoc protocol, the proposed *target-domain BN adaptation (TBA)* strategy achieved 42.69% (1-shot) and 54.24% (5-shot) accuracy.”

14. **Yang et al., 2021, *NeurIPS*** – *Neighborhood Reciprocity Clustering (NRC) for SFDA*. A source-free DA method that enforces **label consistency among local neighbors** in the target embedding space. NRC exploits that same-class target examples cluster together. On standard DA benchmarks, NRC reaches new state-of-the-art; e.g., on VisDA it **surpasses SHOT by 3%** (and supervised source-present baselines). *Relevance:* Improves upon SHOT by leveraging cluster structure, requiring no source data, useful for small labeled lab-trained model deployment. THESIS-QUOTE: “We exploit the intrinsic neighborhood structure of target data: label consistency among local neighbors. NRC significantly outperforms SHOT and reaches state-of-the-art on VisDA and Office-Home, surpassing SHOT by 3% on VisDA.”

15. **Long et al., 2015, *ICML*** – *Deep Adaptation Network (DAN)*. One of the first deep DA methods: embeds task-specific layer outputs in RKHS and minimizes **multi-kernel Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MK-MMD)** between source and target distributions at each layer. This explicitly reduces dataset bias in deep features. DAN showed “state-of-the-art image classification error rates” on standard benchmarks. *Relevance:* Classic feature-matching approach (like CORAL but kernel-based) to bridge lab/field gaps. THESIS-QUOTE: “We propose Deep Adaptation Network (DAN): hidden representations of all task-specific layers are embedded in an RKHS where the mean embeddings of different domains are explicitly matched (via MK-MMD). DAN learns transferable features with statistical guarantees and yields state-of-the-art error rates on standard benchmarks.”

16. **French et al., 2018, *ICLR*** – *Self-Ensembling for Visual Domain Adaptation*. Adopts the “mean teacher” paradigm for DA: a teacher network’s predictions on perturbed target images are used to train the student network, encouraging consistency. It won the VisDA-2017 classification challenge. The paper reports SOTA results on various benchmarks; on small datasets it nearly matches fully supervised accuracy. *Relevance:* Uses unlabeled field images at training time to smooth model outputs, making it robust to shifts. THESIS-QUOTE: “Our approach, derived from the mean-teacher self-ensembling method, achieves state-of-the-art results on a variety of benchmarks, including winning the VISDA-2017 challenge. On small image benchmarks, it outperforms prior art and approaches supervised accuracy.”

17. **Tzeng et al., 2017, *CVPR*** – *Adversarial Discriminative Domain Adaptation (ADDA)*. A two-stage adversarial method: first learn source encoder and classifier, then train a target encoder to fool a domain discriminator using a GAN loss, *without weight-sharing* between encoders. ADDA is simpler than previous methods and achieves strong results. It “exceeds state-of-the-art unsupervised adaptation results” on digit datasets and a cross-modality task. *Relevance:* Another adversarial UDA framework applicable to lab/field image shift, especially when source and target have different data types. THESIS-QUOTE: “We combine discriminative modeling, untied weight sharing, and a GAN loss in a framework called ADDA. ADDA is more effective yet simpler than competing methods, exceeding state-of-the-art UDA results on cross-domain digit classification and other tasks.”

18. **Long et al., 2017, *ICML*** – *Joint Adaptation Networks (JAN)*. Aligns *joint distributions* of multiple domain-specific layers using a **Joint MMD (JMMD)** criterion. JAN extends DAN by measuring distribution discrepancy in the joint feature⊗label space. It even includes an adversarial training step to maximize JMMD for better domain separation. Experiments show JAN yields **state-of-the-art** results on standard DA benchmarks. *Relevance:* More fine-grained alignment (features+predictions jointly), potentially beneficial when background/clutter strongly interacts with class features. THESIS-QUOTE: “We present Joint Adaptation Networks (JAN), which align the joint distributions of multiple task-specific layers across domains using a joint MMD criterion. An adversarial training strategy maximizes JMMD to make source/target more distinguishable, yielding state-of-the-art results.”

19. **Saito et al., 2018, *CVPR*** – *Maximum Classifier Discrepancy (MCD) for UDA*. Proposes two classifiers and a feature generator: after training on source, the classifiers’ disagreement on target inputs is measured, and the generator is trained to minimize this discrepancy. By focusing on target samples near class boundaries, MCD aligns feature spaces more precisely. It “outperforms other methods on several image classification and segmentation datasets”. *Relevance:* Improves decision-boundary alignment, which can help when lab/field differences cause ambiguity. THESIS-QUOTE: “We propose aligning source and target by leveraging task-specific decision boundaries: maximize the discrepancy between two classifiers to detect out-of-support target samples, and train the feature generator to minimize it. Our method outperforms other DA methods on several datasets.”

20. **Cubuk et al., 2019, *CVPR*** – *AutoAugment: Learning Data Augmentation Policies*. Uses reinforcement learning to automatically discover augmentation policies (e.g. translations, color ops) that improve generalization. AutoAugment finds policies that transfer across datasets. It achieves new records: **83.5% top-1** on ImageNet (vs 83.1%) and 1.5% error on CIFAR-10. *Relevance:* Applying learned augmentation to lab images can improve robustness to unknown field variations (lighting, scale, noise). THESIS-QUOTE: “AutoAugment automatically searches for data augmentation strategies. Our method achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, SVHN, and ImageNet: e.g., top-1 ImageNet accuracy of 83.5% (0.4% above prior best).”

# Synthesis by Technique

- **Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (feature alignment):** Includes *DANN*, *DAN*, *DeepCORAL*, *JAN* (all aligning feature distributions via MMD or covariance), and adversarial approaches *ADDA*, *MCD*. These aim to reduce the lab/field gap by making feature representations domain-invariant. For example, CDAN and ADDA use adversarial learning to align features, while DAN and JAN use kernel-based statistical matching. Empirical results (e.g. ~90% Office accuracy by CDAN) suggest these methods significantly boost cross-domain performance.

- **Source-Free Domain Adaptation:** Methods that need only a pretrained source model and unlabeled target data. Key examples are *SHOT* and *NRC*. SHOT avoids source images entirely by aligning target features to the frozen source classifier (achieving strong results). NRC further uses target sample clusters (neighbor reciprocity) to improve upon SHOT. These are very relevant when lab images are proprietary and only a model is deployed in-field.

- **Test-Time Adaptation / Training:** Methods adapting on-the-fly to incoming target data. *Tent* and *TTT* exemplify this. Tent changes model statistics by minimizing entropy during test, while TTT retrains on each sample via self-supervision. They require no retraining on source or target beforehand. These are attractive for field deployment where rapid adaptation to new conditions is needed without offline retraining.

- **Domain Generalization / Robustness:** Techniques aimed at training models to generalize to unseen domains. *IRM* and *GroupDRO* enforce invariance or worst-case performance over multiple training domains. While they assume multiple “source” environments, their principles (e.g. invariance or robustness to group shifts) can inspire robust lab training. *AugMix*, *AutoAugment*, and *AdaBN* are augmentation/statistic techniques to improve model robustness to shifts. Domain randomization is also in this spirit, artificially varying lab images so real-field images appear as in-distribution.

- **Style Transfer / Augmentation:** Methods like *CyCADA* perform pixel-level style transfer (adversarially) to make lab images look field-like, and histogram/color normalization can reduce style gap. Data-augmentation strategies (*AugMix*, *AutoAugment*, *Randomization*) help cover field variability.

- **Retrieval/Metric-Based Approaches:** Not explicitly in these papers, but methods like $k$-NN or prototypical classifiers on frozen features could be used: e.g., it’s known that $k$-NN on robust features can outperform softmax under domain shift. (No specific paper above, but consider as alternative.)

- **BatchNorm Calibration:** Techniques like *AdaBN* or Zhu et al.’s *Target BN Adaptation* show that aligning feature statistics (means/vars) at training or test time can dramatically reduce the lab-field gap.

- **Agricultural / Lab-to-Field Case Studies:** The special-case works include Zhao et al. (2026), which directly studies the PlantVillage→PlantDoc scenario and finds simple BN recalibration (in an EfficientNet) achieves a majority of the gain. This highlights that even on small agricultural datasets, domain adaptation ideas (BN alignment, etc.) apply.

# Recommended Papers for Review

- **Y. Ganin and V. Lempitsky (2016), JMLR**: *Domain-Adversarial Training of Neural Networks*. A seminal work introducing gradient-reversal domain adaptation. High citation count.
- **J. Hoffman et al. (2018), ICML**: *CyCADA: Cycle-Consistent Adversarial Domain Adaptation*. Classic pixel-level + feature alignment.
- **G. French et al. (2018), ICLR**: *Self-Ensembling for Visual DA*. Demonstrated best results on VisDA challenge.
- **D. Liang et al. (2020), ICML**: *SHOT: Source Hypothesis Transfer*. Introduced source-free DA.
- **D. Wang et al. (2021), ICLR**: *Tent: Fully Test-Time Adaptation*. Top performance on corrupted ImageNet.
- **R. Sagawa et al. (2020), ICML**: *GroupDRO*. Important concept of worst-case robustness.
- **Y. Arjovsky et al. (2020), ICLR**: *Invariant Risk Minimization*. Foundational domain-generalization theory.
- **Z. Saito et al. (2018), CVPR**: *Maximum Classifier Discrepancy*. Effective UDA via classifier divergence.
